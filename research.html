Working Papers

Transmission of Climate Risk in Corporate Loans (with Max Yang)
Abstract

Natural disasters are geographically concentrated, but corporate credit markets are integrated through national financial intermediaries, raising the question of whether local shocks propagate through lending networks. Using U.S. syndicated loan data around Hurricane Harvey (August 2017), we compare lenders with different pre-storm portfolio concentration in the damaged counties lending to the same unaffected borrower. Lenders one standard deviation more exposed to Harvey cut credit to unaffected borrowers by 4.8 percent, with no adjustment in spreads or maturities, even for borrowers more than 1,000 miles from the impact zone. Instead, the borrower's hurricane risk, measured by the historical frequency of hurricanes at its location, predicts the contraction: credit falls in hurricane-prone counties, exposed lenders move about two percentage points of their portfolios out of risky counties, and they are more likely to end relationships with hurricane-risky borrowers. The evidence is consistent with a highly visible disaster leading exposed lenders to move away from climate-risky borrowers throughout their portfolios, a reallocation of climate risk in quantities.

Ex-post Evaluation of the R&D Tax Incentive Law in Chile (with Cristian Mardones)
Abstract

This study carries out an ex - post evaluation of the Research and Development (R&D) Tax Incentive Law in Chile. A subset of data from the 9th Innovation Survey is chosen, specifically those that were available for the years 2013 and 2014. Then, differences in differences (DID) and matching with differences in differences (MDID) techniques are used to identify the impact of this policy. The results obtained allow us to affirm that the use of the R&D Tax Incentive Law had some positive but very low effects on some components of expenditure for innovation. Also, the positive effect of the tax credits on the total expenditure for innovation identified with MDID disappears when only firms that know the R&D Tax Incentive Law are used as a control group. This work provides new evidence to evaluate innovation policies in Latin America, focusing on tax credits that have been much less studied than subsidy programs. Specifically, it is concluded that there is still a wide margin to improve and reformulate the R&D Tax Incentive Law in Chile.
